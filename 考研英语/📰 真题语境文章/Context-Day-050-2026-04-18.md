## English Text

The transformation of rural landscapes across the developing world has become one of the defining **movement**s of the twenty-first century. Where once a **peasant** family tended a small **orchard** of **peach** trees or grazed cattle on a humble **pasture**, today entire regions are being reshaped by the convergence of environmental science, economic policy, and grassroots activism. The change is visible from the air: a **passenger** gazing out the window of a **plane** can observe how patchwork fields of **onion** and **peanut** have given way to vast, mechanised farms. It is a revolution that promises **plenty** — but only if it is managed wisely.

At the heart of this transformation lies a growing understanding of soil **physiology** and atmospheric chemistry. Scientists have demonstrated that excessive use of synthetic fertilisers disrupts the balance of **nitrogen** and **oxygen** in the earth, producing harmful **oxide** compounds that degrade both land and water. In one landmark study, a **physicist** collaborated with agricultural researchers to measure how emissions from a single **oven** in a rural household, burning **oil** and **coal**, could contribute to localised pollution. The findings were alarming: even the **ore** extracted from nearby hills carried traces of heavy metals that leached into the soil beneath a once-thriving **meadow**. Such research has lent urgency to the global sustainability **movement**, pushing governments to launch legislative **offensive**s against environmental negligence.

The human dimension of rural change is equally complex. In village after village, young people have **pick**ed up and left for the cities, drawn by the lure of employment in **museum**s, **magazine** publishing houses, and technology firms. Those who remain are **plenty** aware of the challenges. An ageing **peasant** may **pine** for the days when neighbours gathered at the **playground** after a long day's work, sharing **meal**s under the shade of an **oak** tree. Yet nostalgia alone cannot sustain a community. In some regions, the population decline has been so severe that a once-bustling **palace** of local governance — the village committee hall — now sits empty, its doors shut, its windows dark. Neither government subsidies **nor** charitable foundations have fully arrested the decline.

In response, a new generation of rural entrepreneurs has begun to **piece** together innovative business models. Some have converted abandoned **pine** forests into eco-tourism destinations, where visitors from the city can sleep on a simple **pillow** in a rustic cabin, hike along a **pebble**-lined trail, and learn about sustainable agriculture. Others have turned to speciality crops: **melon** and **pepper** grown in polytunnels, **mushroom** cultivation that has **mushroom**ed into a lucrative export industry. A **manager** at one such cooperative reports that revenues have climbed to an impressive **per cent**age above the regional average. These enterprises are small, but collectively they represent a meaningful **plan** for rural revitalisation.

Not everyone, however, welcomes the changes. A **plaintiff** in a recent court case argued that a corporate farm's use of pesticides had contaminated the local water supply, affecting **patient**s at the nearby clinic who were already struggling with respiratory illness. The **party** representing the villagers demanded stricter regulation, while the defence countered that the scientific evidence was inconclusive. It was a **pity**, observed one **nurse** who had tended to the sick for decades, that economic progress so often came at the expense of public health. She had watched children play near **pink** wildflowers by the stream, unaware that the water they splashed in might carry invisible toxins.

The science of sustainability demands patience and rigour. Researchers studying the carbon cycle note that a single mature **oak** can absorb **mountain**s of carbon dioxide over its lifetime, while the loss of **orchard** land to urban sprawl releases stored carbon back into the atmosphere. **Mathematics** and **physics** models are essential tools for predicting these dynamics, yet the data remain imperfect. A study published in a leading scientific **magazine** found that estimates of soil carbon sequestration varied by as much as thirty **percent** depending on the measurement method employed. Such uncertainty makes it difficult for policymakers to design effective interventions, but it should not serve as an excuse for inaction. As one researcher put it, we must not let the perfect become the enemy of the good.

The path forward requires cooperation across every **plane** of society — from the individual **person** who chooses to buy locally grown produce, to the **neighbour** who shares tools and knowledge, to the national governments that must fund research and enforce environmental protections. The stakes are **plenty** high. If current trends continue, the rural landscapes that have sustained human civilisation for millennia may be irreversibly damaged. But if the lessons of science are heeded, and if communities are given the resources they need to adapt, there is reason for hope. The transformation of the countryside need not be a tragedy; with careful stewardship, it can be a story of renewal.

## 中文译文

发展中国家乡村景观的转型已成为二十一世纪最具决定性的**运动**（movement）之一。曾经，一个**农民**（peasant）家庭照料着一片**桃**（peach）树**果园**（orchard），或在简陋的**牧场**（pasture）上放牛；如今，整个地区正被环境科学、经济政策和基层行动主义的融合所重塑。这种变化从空中就能看到：一位**乘客**（passenger**）从**飞机**（plane）的窗口向外望去，可以观察到**洋葱**（onion）和**花生**（peanut）的拼块田地如何让位于广袤的机械化农场。这是一场承诺带来**丰裕**（plenty）的革命——但前提是必须明智地加以管理。

这一转型的核心在于人们对土壤**生理学**（physiology）和大气化学的认识不断加深。科学家们已经证明，过度使用合成肥料会破坏土壤中**氮**（nitrogen）和**氧**（oxygen）的平衡，产生有害的**氧化物**（oxide）化合物，从而降解土地和水质。在一项具有里程碑意义的研究中，一位**物理学家**（physicist）与农业研究人员合作，测量了农村家庭中单个使用**油**（oil）和煤炭的**炉灶**（oven）的排放如何造成局部污染。研究结果令人担忧：甚至从附近山丘开采的**矿石**（ore）也携带着重金属痕迹，渗入曾经繁茂的**草甸**（meadow）下方的土壤中。此类研究为全球可持续发展**运动**（movement）增添了紧迫感，促使各国政府发起针对环境疏忽的立法**攻势**（offensive）。

乡村变迁中人的层面同样复杂。在一个又一个村庄里，年轻人**选择**（pick）离开前往城市，被**博物馆**（museum**）里、**杂志**（magazine**）出版公司里以及科技企业中的就业机会所**诱惑**（lure）。留下来的人们**非常**（plenty）清楚这些挑战。一位年迈的**农民**（peasant**）可能会**怀念**（pine）过去邻居们在**操场**（playground）上劳作一天后聚在一起、在**橡树**（oak）荫下共享**饭菜**（meal）的日子。然而仅靠怀旧无法支撑一个社区。在一些地区，人口下降如此严重，以至于曾经热闹的地方治理**殿堂**（palace）——村民委员会大厅——现在空无一人，大门紧闭，窗户昏暗。政府补贴和慈善基金会**都未能**（neither...nor...）完全遏制这种衰退。

作为回应，新一代农村企业家开始**拼合**（piece）创新的商业模式。一些人将废弃的**松树**（pine）林改造为生态旅游目的地，来自城市的游客可以在乡村小屋里枕着简易的**枕头**（pillow）入睡，沿着铺满**鹅卵石**（pebble）的小径徒步，学习可持续农业知识。另一些人转向特色作物：在塑料大棚中种植的**甜瓜**（melon）和**辣椒**（pepper），以及已**迅速增长**（mushroom）为利润丰厚出口产业的**蘑菇**（mushroom）种植。一家此类合作社的**经理**（manager）报告说，收入已攀升至高于地区平均水平的可观**百分比**（per cent）。这些企业规模虽小，但集体而言，它们代表了一项有意义的乡村振兴**计划**（plan）。

然而，并非所有人都欢迎这些变化。近期一起诉讼中的**原告**（plaintiff）指出，一家企业农场使用农药污染了当地水源，影响了附近诊所的**病人**（patient），这些**病人**本就饱受呼吸道疾病之苦。代表村民的**一方**（party）要求加强监管，而辩方则反驳说科学证据尚不确凿。一位照料病人长达数十年的**护士**（nurse**）感慨说，经济进步往往以公共健康为代价，实在令人**惋惜**（pity）。她曾看着孩子们在溪边**粉色的**（pink）野花旁玩耍，浑然不知他们溅起的水花可能携带着看不见的毒素。

可持续发展科学需要耐心和严谨。研究碳循环的研究人员指出，一棵成熟的**橡树**（oak**）在其一生中可以吸收**大量的**（mountain，比喻义）二氧化碳，而**果园**（orchard**）用地因城市扩张而流失则会将储存的碳释放回大气中。**数学**（mathematics）和**物理学**（physics**）模型是预测这些动态的重要工具，但数据仍然不完善。发表在一本领先科学**杂志**（magazine**）上的一项研究发现，根据所采用的测量方法，土壤碳封存量的估算差异高达三十**个百分点**（percent）。这种不确定性使得决策者难以设计有效的干预措施，但这不应成为不作为的借口。正如一位研究者所言，我们不能让完美成为好的敌人。

前进的道路需要社会各个**层面**（plane）的合作——从选择购买本地农产品的每个**人**（person），到分享工具和知识的**邻居**（neighbour），到必须资助研究并执行环境保护的国家政府。风险**相当**（plenty）之高。如果目前的趋势继续下去，维系人类文明数千年的乡村景观可能会遭受不可逆转的破坏。但如果科学经验得到重视，如果社区获得适应所需的资源，就有理由抱有希望。乡村的转型未必是一场悲剧；在精心管理之下，它可以成为一部复兴的故事。
