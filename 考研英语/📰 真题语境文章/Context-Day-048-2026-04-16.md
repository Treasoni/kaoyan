# 📰 真题语境文章 - Day 048

**日期**: 2026-04-16
**主题**: The Rise and Fall of Fossil Cities: Urban Sustainability in Question (化石城市的兴衰：城市可持续性的拷问)
**使用词汇**: 57个本日单词

---

## English Text

The relationship between human civilization and energy consumption has long been a subject of intense **discourse** among scholars. As early as the Industrial Revolution, when coal-powered **furnaces** replaced wood-burning stoves in countless **dwellings**, humanity embarked on a path of **excessive** resource exploitation that continues to this day. The **founders** of modern industry could hardly have imagined the **difficulty** we now face in reversing this trend.

Consider the **different** layers of the problem. **During** the 20th century, entire cities were designed around the automobile. Multi-lane highways cut through **forests** and neighborhoods alike, while **gasoline** consumption soared to unprecedented levels. A single American household might burn through a **dozen** **gallons** of fuel each week simply commuting from suburban **dormitories** to downtown offices. The **famous** urban planner Robert Moses **directed** the construction of highways that prioritized vehicles over pedestrians, fundamentally altering the **landscape** — and the **fate** — of American cities.

The environmental consequences are impossible to ignore. Rising temperatures, driven by the burning of **fossil** fuels, have led to more frequent **earthquakes** in previously stable regions and dramatic **eclipses** of air quality in major cities. **Farmers** struggle with unpredictable weather patterns, forcing them to apply more **fertilizer** per acre just to maintain crop yields. The **dust** from degraded farmland blows into urban areas, settling on **furniture** and clothing, on every **garment** and **floor** and **doorway** — a tangible reminder of ecological collapse.

Yet the story is not entirely grim. **Electronic** innovations have begun to reshape how we power our lives. The **electron**, once a theoretical curiosity confined to physics **dictionaries**, now drives the global transition toward renewable energy. **Electricity** generated from solar panels and wind turbines is gradually replacing **gasoline** in transportation. In China, a **fleet** of electric buses has already transformed urban transit, and **exciting** advances in battery technology promise to accelerate this shift.

Some cities have taken **direct** action. Copenhagen, for instance, has set an **excellent** example by promoting cycling culture. Residents park their bicycles not behind locked **gates** but in open racks, and the city's extensive network of bike lanes ensures that a **few** minutes of pedaling can replace a car trip. The annual cycling **festival** celebrates this commitment, drawing visitors from around the world who come to witness a city that has chosen sustainability over convenience.

The transition, however, is not without its **difficult** choices. Developing nations argue that they cannot be expected to abandon cheap energy sources while their **elders** still remember when **electricity** was a luxury. An **emperor** of industry in the fossil fuel sector may argue that **expensive** renewable infrastructure is beyond the reach of poorer nations. This **dialogue** between developed and developing worlds continues to **fox** negotiators at every international climate summit.

Meanwhile, ordinary citizens face their own **examinations** of conscience. Every purchase at the **fridge** aisle — every item wrapped in plastic, every out-of-season product shipped across oceans — carries an ecological cost. The **envelope** of greenhouse gases thickens year by year. Scientists urge us not to **forget** that the window for meaningful action is closing. We must **enjoy** the comforts of modern life without **destroying** the natural systems that sustain us, or else we risk turning our shared **dream** of a green future into a permanent nightmare.

## 中文译文

人类文明与能源消耗之间的关系长期以来一直是学者们激烈**讨论**（discourse）的主题。早在工业革命时期，当燃煤**熔炉**（furnaces）取代了无数**住宅**（dwellings）中的柴火炉时，人类就踏上了一条**过度的**（excessive）资源开发之路，这一趋势延续至今。现代工业的**缔造者**（founders）几乎无法想象我们如今在逆转这一趋势方面所面临的**困难**（difficulty）。

从**不同的**（different）层面来审视这个问题。**在**（during）20世纪期间，整个城市都是围绕汽车设计的。多车道高速公路穿过**森林**（forests）和居民区，而**汽油**（gasoline）消耗量飙升至前所未有的水平。一个美国家庭每周可能仅从郊区**宿舍式住宅**（dormitories）通勤到市中心办公室就消耗掉**十二**（dozen）**加仑**（gallons）的燃料。**著名的**（famous）城市规划师罗伯特·摩西**主导**（directed）了优先考虑车辆而非行人的高速公路建设，从根本上改变了美国城市的面貌——以及命运。

环境后果是无法忽视的。由**化石**（fossil）燃料燃烧驱动的气温上升，导致以前稳定地区更频繁地发生**地震**（earthquakes），大城市空气质量的急剧**黯然失色**（eclipses）。**农民**（farmers）在不可预测的天气模式中挣扎，不得不每英亩施用更多**肥料**（fertilizer）以维持作物产量。退化农田的**灰尘**（dust）吹入城区，落在**家具**（furniture）和衣物上，落在每一件**衣服**（garment）、每一块**地板**（floor）和每一个**门口**（doorway）——这是生态崩溃的切实提醒。

然而，情况并非完全黯淡。**电子的**（electronic）创新已经开始重塑我们的能源方式。曾经局限于物理**词典**（dictionaries）的理论好奇——**电子**（electron），如今驱动着全球向可再生能源的转型。太阳能电池板和风力涡轮机产生的**电力**（electricity）正在逐渐取代交通运输中的**汽油**（gasoline）。在中国，一支**车队**（fleet）的电动公交车已经改变了城市交通，电池技术的**令人振奋的**（exciting）进展有望加速这一转变。

一些城市已经采取了**直接的**（direct）行动。例如，哥本哈根通过推广骑行文化树立了一个**优秀的**（excellent）榜样。居民将自行车停放在开放式车架上，而非锁在**大门**（gates）之后，城市广泛的自行车道网络确保**少数**（few）分钟的骑行就能替代一次汽车出行。年度骑行**节**（festival）庆祝这一承诺，吸引来自世界各地的游客，亲眼见证一座选择可持续性而非便利性的城市。

然而，这一转型并非没有**艰难的**（difficult）抉择。发展中国家争辩说，不能期望他们放弃廉价的能源来源，而他们的**长辈**（elders）还记得**电力**（electricity）曾是奢侈品的时代。化石燃料行业的一位行业**巨头**（emperor）可能会争辩说，**昂贵的**（expensive）可再生基础设施超出了较贫穷国家的能力。发达国家与发展中国家之间的这种**对话**（dialogue）在每一次国际气候峰会上都继续**困扰**（fox）谈判代表。

与此同时，普通市民也面临着自身的良心**考验**（examinations）。在**冰箱**（fridge）货架旁的每一次购买——每一件塑料包装的物品，每一个跨洋运输的反季节产品——都带有生态成本。温室气体的**外层包裹**（envelope）年复一年地增厚。科学家敦促我们不要**忘记**（forget），采取有意义的行动窗口正在关闭。我们必须在**享受**（enjoy）现代生活舒适的同时，不去**破坏**（destroying）维系我们的自然系统，否则我们就有可能将我们共同的绿色未来之**梦**（dream）变成永久的噩梦。

> 3. 重点关注 ⚠️ 僻义标注的词汇：
>    - **found** 作为"创办/建立"之意，过去式为 founded，注意与 find 的过去式 found 区分
>    - **direct** 在文中作动词"主导/指挥"，与形容词"直接的"为同形异义
>    - **fox** 作动词意为"难住/使困惑"，是典型的名词动词化用法
>    - **eclipse** 作动词"使黯然失色"，常用于比喻某事物被另一事物掩盖
>    - **envelope** 此处非"信封"，而是"包裹层"（科学用语，如 envelope of gases）
