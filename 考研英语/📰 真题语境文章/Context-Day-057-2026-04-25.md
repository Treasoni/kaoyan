## Passage: Will Power: The Quiet Force Behind Institutional Accountability

The **world** of public governance has **usually** been shaped by an **usual** assumption: that institutions can regulate **themselves** without external oversight. Yet **history** tells a different story. When leaders **abdicate** their responsibilities, the consequences can be devastating — **war**, corruption, and the erosion of public trust are **too** often the result.

Consider the case of a **young** **university** professor who dared to make an **accusation** against a powerful institution. Dr. Sarah Chen, an **admirable** scholar in **academia**, published a **study** revealing that the organization had been misusing public funds. Her research showed that benefits that should have **accrued** to local communities had instead been diverted to private interests. The **abbreviation** "CFO" — Chief Financial Officer — became synonymous **with** scandal in the local media.

The **trouble** began when the institution's leadership attempted to **weather** the storm through denial rather than reform. "There is **no** **problem** **whatsoever** with our financial practices," declared the CEO, whose **very** position was **under** scrutiny. But **under** the surface, the **word** was spreading. **Someone** had leaked internal documents, and they painted a troubling picture.

The **thin** veneer of respectability began to crack. The public learned that the institution had operated **without** meaningful **accountability** for years. Its leaders had ignored the **will** of the people, acting as if they were **absolutists** rather than public servants. "They **think** they can do whatever they want," said one frustrated citizen. "But **unless** there are consequences, nothing will change."

**What** happened next demonstrated the power of collective action. Citizens organized a **peaceful** protest on a **Wednesday** **evening**, gathering in the **street** outside the institution's headquarters. The protest grew throughout the **week**, attracting **young** and old alike. By **Thursday**, local television stations were covering the story extensively. The **weather** that **week** was terrible — cold, rain, and wind — but the protesters **would** not be deterred.

The movement's **wish** was simple: transparency and **accountability**. They demanded that the institution **use** its resources for their intended purpose. They wanted leaders who were **thirsty** for justice rather than power. "We **welcome** this scrutiny," said a **well**-known community organizer. "It is **about time** that someone spoke up."

**When** the investigation finally concluded, the findings were damning. The institution had systematically evaded oversight, **usually** by exploiting legal loopholes. Several officials were forced to step down. The **wrong** they had committed was not merely financial — it was a betrayal of public trust.

The aftermath proved that institutions can indeed reform when citizens refuse to **stay** silent. New policies ensured greater **accessibility** of financial records. The **adornment** of titles and privileges gave way to genuine **accountability**. As Dr. Chen noted in a subsequent interview, "Power without oversight is like a ship without a rudder. It takes citizens who are **willing** to speak truth to power to keep the ship on course."

Today, the case is **usually** cited as a textbook example of how institutional reform can be achieved through civic engagement. The **world** is watching, and the **lesson** is clear: the **will** of the people, when properly channeled, can **weather** any storm.

## 重点词汇语境解析

### 1. will (n. 意志/意愿)
> The movement's **wish** was simple... ignored the **will** of the people...

**考研用法**：will 作名词"意志/意愿"，如 the will of the people（人民的意愿），free will（自由意志），strong will（坚强意志）。

### 2. word (n. 消息)
> ...the **word** was spreading.

**考研用法**：word 作"消息"，不加冠词，如 Word has it that...（有消息说...）。

### 3. under (prep. 在...控制下)
> ...whose very position was **under** scrutiny.

**考研用法**：under + 抽象名词表示"在...过程中/控制下"，如 under investigation（在调查中），under construction（在建设中）。

### 4. weather (v. 经受住)
> ...attempted to **weather** the storm through denial...

**考研用法**：weather the storm = 平安渡过风暴/困难，是考研高频搭配。

### 5. abdicate (v. 放弃)
> When leaders **abdicate** their responsibilities...

**考研用法**：abdicate responsibility = 放弃责任，比 give up 更正式。

### 6. accusation (n. 指控)
> ...make an **accusation** against a powerful institution.

**考研用法**：make an accusation against = 对...提出指控，同义替换 charge/allegation。

### 7. accountability (n. 责任心)
> ...meaningful **accountibility** for years.

**考研用法**：accountability 是考研高频词，常出现在政府/企业管理话题中，强调"问责制"。

### 8. accrue (v. 积累)
> ...benefits that should have **accrued** to local communities...

**考研用法**：accrue to = 积累到/惠及，如 benefits accrue to the public（利益惠及公众）。

### 9. thirsty (adj. 渴望的)
> ...**thirsty** for justice rather than power.

**考研用法**：thirsty for = 渴望，比 eager/hungry 更有文学色彩。

### 10. trouble (n. 问题/麻烦)
> The **trouble** began when...

**考研用法**：trouble 引出问题所在，常见句式 The trouble is that...（问题在于...）。

## 长难句解析

### 句1
> The thin veneer of respectability began to crack when the public learned that the institution had operated without meaningful accountability for years.

**结构分析**：
- 主干：The thin veneer began to crack
- 时间状语从句：when the public learned that...
- 宾语从句：that the institution had operated without meaningful accountability
- 介词短语：for years

**翻译**：当公众得知该机构多年来一直在没有有效问责的情况下运作时，那层薄薄的体面外衣开始破裂。

### 句2
> They demanded that the institution use its resources for their intended purpose.

**结构分析**：
- 主干：They demanded that...
- 虚拟语气：that the institution (should) use...（demand 后的宾语从句用虚拟语气，should 可省略）

**翻译**：他们要求该机构将其资源用于预期的目的。
