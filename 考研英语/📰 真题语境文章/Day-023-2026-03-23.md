## 📖 原文

### Urban Renewal and Social Transformation

In recent decades, metropolitan areas across the globe have undergone remarkable transformation. The Renaissance of urban ==districts==, once dismissed as **ordinary** and **flat** in character, has become a **fixture** of modern city planning. Municipal authorities have sought to **fix** deteriorating infrastructure, **postpone** inevitable costs no longer being an option.

The **portable** technologies of the digital age have **fostered** a new class of workers—professionals who can work from **anywhere**, unbound by **any** specific location. This shift has **profoundly** altered the **orientation** of urban economies. Real estate developers now **devote** substantial resources to creating mixed-use spaces, their investment **portfolios** reflecting changing demands.

However, this transformation is **anything but** smooth. Critics argue that rapid gentrification has **blazed** through historic neighborhoods, **displacing** longtime residents who **flee** rising rents. The **flame** of progress, they contend, often **consumes** the very communities it claims to help. Community advocates have **repeatedly** raised concerns, their voices a **portrait** of frustration with policies that favor profit over people.

Urban planners must **address** these concerns directly. The **posture** of city officials toward displacement matters significantly. Some have adopted an **orderly** approach, establishing **metrics** to measure social impact before approving **any** major development. Others have been **blamed** for prioritizing quick profits over sustainable growth.

The challenge lies in balancing competing interests. On one hand, cities must **renovate** aging infrastructure and **repair** crumbling facilities. The **remnants** of industrial pasts must be repurposed. On the other hand, they must ensure that longtime residents—**porters**, maintenance workers, and service staff—can afford to remain in the neighborhoods they helped build.

Technology offers some solutions. Advanced **detectors** monitor air quality and noise levels. **Digital portfolios** showcase neighborhood histories. **Metropolitan** planners use sophisticated **meters** to track traffic patterns and optimize public transit. Yet technology alone cannot solve the **bind** that many low-income residents face.

The **assignment** for urban leaders is clear: create cities that are both dynamic and inclusive. This requires more than **quick fixes** or **flash** initiatives. It demands sustained commitment to policies that **repay** the social trust invested in public institutions. The **merit** of any urban policy should be judged not merely by economic **metrics** but by its capacity to preserve community bonds.

Some cities have begun to **exemplify** this balanced approach. Rather than **snatch** properties from reluctant sellers, they offer fair compensation and relocation assistance. Rather than **sneak** unpopular decisions through bureaucratic channels, they engage citizens in genuine dialogue. The **flavor** of democratic participation enriches the final outcome.

Yet challenges persist. The **tiresome** debates over zoning regulations continue. The **snobbish** attitudes of some newcomers toward established residents create friction. The **tissue** of community trust, once **torn**, proves difficult to **repair**.

Perhaps the solution lies in reimagining the very **metaphor** of urban renewal. Rather than viewing cities as machines requiring periodic **blasts** of investment, we might see them as living organisms—complex systems where every **blade** of grass, every **porcelain** storefront, every resident matters. The **orbit** of influence extends beyond property lines.

The **merry** bustle of diverse neighborhoods, where different cultures **merge** and evolve, represents the true **spirit** of urban life. This **nonsense** notion that cities must choose between preservation and progress presents a false dichotomy. With thoughtful **orientation** toward both past and future, cities can **sustain** their essential character while embracing necessary change.

## 📖 参考译文

### 城市更新与社会变迁

近几十年来，全球大都市地区经历了显著的转型。曾经被认为是**普通**和**平淡**的城市街区，如今已成为现代城市规划的**固定特色**，焕发着**文艺复兴**般的新生。市政当局已着手**修理**日益恶化的基础设施，**延期**支付不可避免的成本已不再是选项。

数字时代的**便携式**技术催生了新一代劳动者——这些专业人士可以在**任何地方**工作，不受**任何**特定地点的束缚。这种转变**深刻地**改变了城市经济的**方向**。房地产开发商现在**投入**大量资源创造混合用途空间，其投资**组合**反映了不断变化的需求。

然而，这种转型**绝非**一帆风顺。批评者认为，快速的中产阶级化如烈火般**席卷**历史街区，**迫使**因租金上涨而**逃离**的老居民流离失所。他们 contend，进步的**火焰**往往**吞噬**了它声称要帮助的社区。社区倡导者**再三**表达关切，他们的声音是受挫情绪的**写照**，表达了对重利轻人政策的失望。

城市规划者必须直接**处理**这些关切。市政官员对流离失所问题的**态度**至关重要。一些人采取了**有序**的方法，在批准**任何**重大开发项目之前建立**衡量指标**来评估社会影响。另一些人则因将快速获利置于可持续增长之上而受到**责备**。

挑战在于平衡相互竞争的利益。一方面，城市必须**翻新**老化的基础设施，**修复**破败的设施。工业历史的**残余**必须被重新利用。另一方面，他们必须确保长期居民——**搬运工**、维修工人和服务人员——能够负担得起留在他们帮助建设的社区。

技术提供了一些解决方案。先进的**探测器**监测空气质量和噪音水平。**数字作品集**展示社区历史。**大都市**规划者使用精密的**计量器**追踪交通模式并优化公共交通。然而，仅靠技术无法解决许多低收入居民面临的**困境**。

城市领导者的**任务**很明确：创造既有活力又包容的城市。这需要的不仅仅是**权宜之计**或**昙花一现**的举措。它要求对政策保持持续承诺，**回报**公众对公共机构的社会信任。任何城市政策的**价值**不应仅以经济**指标**来评判，而应以其维护社区纽带的能力来衡量。

一些城市已经开始**体现**这种平衡方法。它们不是从 reluctant 的卖家手中**夺取**房产，而是提供公平的补偿和搬迁援助。它们不是通过官僚渠道**偷偷**推行不受欢迎的决定，而是让公民参与真正的对话。民主参与的**风味**丰富了最终结果。

然而挑战依然存在。关于分区法规的**令人厌倦**的辩论仍在继续。一些新来者对老居民的**势利**态度制造了摩擦。社区信任的**组织**一旦被撕裂，就很难**修复**。

也许解决方案在于重新构想城市更新的**隐喻**。与其将城市视为需要定期**爆破**投资的机器，我们不如将它们视为有生命的有机体——每一个**草叶**、每一家**瓷器**店、每一位居民都很重要的复杂系统。影响的**轨道**延伸到地界线之外。

不同文化**融合**和发展的多元街区的**欢乐**喧嚣，代表了城市生活的真正精神。认为城市必须在保护与进步之间做出选择是一种**荒谬**观念，提出了一个错误的二分法。通过对过去和未来的深思熟虑的**定向**，城市可以在拥抱必要变化的同时**维持**其本质特征。

## 📖 阅读理解练习

### Questions

1. According to the passage, what has contributed to the transformation of urban economies?

A. The decline of metropolitan areas
B. The rise of portable technologies and remote work
C. The postponement of infrastructure repairs
D. The reduction of investment portfolios

2. The phrase "anything but" in paragraph 3 most nearly means:

A. Everything except
B. Nothing more than
C. Far from being
D. Something like

3. Critics of rapid gentrification argue that it:

A. Benefits longtime residents economically
B. Creates more orderly neighborhoods
C. Displaces residents who cannot afford rising rents
D. Improves the flavor of local communities

4. The author suggests that the merit of urban policy should be judged by:

A. Economic metrics alone
B. Its capacity to preserve community bonds
C. The speed of renovation projects
D. The size of investment portfolios

5. What metaphor does the author propose for understanding cities?

A. Machines requiring periodic investment
B. Living organisms with complex systems
C. Portraits of historical change
D. Portfolios of real estate assets

### 答案与解析

**1. B** - 文中第二段提到 "The portable technologies of the digital age have fostered a new class of workers—professionals who can work from anywhere"，说明便携技术和远程工作推动了城市经济转型。

**2. C** - "anything but" 意为"绝非，根本不"。文中 "this transformation is anything but smooth" 意为"这种转型绝非一帆风顺"。

**3. C** - 第三段明确指出 "displacing longtime residents who flee rising rents"，批评者认为快速中产阶级化导致因租金上涨而被迫离开的老居民流离失所。

**4. B** - 倒数第五段提到 "The merit of any urban policy should be judged not merely by economic metrics but by its capacity to preserve community bonds"，强调应以维护社区纽带的能力来评判。

**5. B** - 倒数第三段提出 "we might see them as living organisms—complex systems where every blade of grass, every porcelain storefront, every resident matters"，作者建议将城市视为有生命的有机体。
